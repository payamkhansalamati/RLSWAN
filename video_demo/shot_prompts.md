# Cinematic footage prompts and assembly contract

These are ready production prompts, not claims that footage was generated. Reuse the actual saved worker, robot and environment references as visual conditioning on every applicable generation. Keep the same fictional adult face, hairstyle, teal scrubs, plain badge, robot proportions, display, corridor geometry and daylight throughout. No patients, records, readable badges, medical data, real hospital branding or extra staff. Landscape 16:9; request 1920×1080 where supported and at least 24 fps. Use only existing authorized credits.

## Shared visual direction

Photoreal contemporary hospital logistics corridor, broad clear circulation space, soft natural daylight from side windows, clean pale walls, restrained teal details, realistic contact shadows and cinematic neutral grading. One fictional adult healthcare worker in teal scrubs; natural skin, believable eyes and hands, subtle acting. One compact practical white-and-teal wheeled supply robot with secure compartment, black front display, no face or cartoon eyes. Natural camera movement and physically plausible wheel rotation. No generated text, graphics, glowing scanning beams or diagnostic visualization. Display remains a dark blank surface reserved for deterministic post-production lettering. No generated speech is retained.

For monitor shots: frame the display nearly front-on, steady camera with no zoom, robot stationary, screen unobstructed, all four screen corners visible for the entire take; reserve bottom 190 pixels of the final 1080p composition for subtitles. Reflections are subdued. The reference display is about 2.45:1, a wide rectangle. For readable wide-screen summaries, frame it around 1100–1300 output pixels wide in true close-ups; a 650-pixel screen would make small training annotations difficult to read. Screen corner coordinates must be measured after normalization; the template does not invent them. A drifting screen requires measured tracking data or regeneration, not a fixed overlay that slips across the chassis.

## Required generation order and review gates

Generate and measure final ElevenLabs speech first. Then produce one worker speaking/lip-sync test and one stable robot-monitor test. Inspect the tests at 100% delivery scale before spending credits on the remaining footage. Reuse successful references; cache accepted clips. Review mouth synchronization, facial identity, fingers, wheel contact, screen stability and readable composite text. Generation is blocked until the account has the necessary video/lip-sync capabilities; do not buy or upgrade a plan automatically.

## Reproducible assembly

All paths are relative to `video_demo/timeline.json`. `source_clips/` holds actual generated or filmed motion clips; `dialogue/` holds final uncompressed PCM WAV speech. `clips` contains `id`, `path`, timeline `start`, `duration`, `source_in`, `source_kind` (`generated_video` or `live_action`), and either `monitor_quad: null` or four `[x,y]` points in **TL, TR, BR, BL** order in the normalized 1920×1080 output. Only stable quads are accepted by this version. Use a stable take or extend the compositor with measured per-frame tracking if the monitor moves. Never apply guessed corners.

`requires_lipsync: true` shots need `lipsync_verified: true` after inspection. `needs_monitor_quad: true` prevents export while corners are missing. Both the WORKING cut at 37–40 and the explain shot require actual measured monitor corners. The current 60-second edit accommodates actual generated speech but remains subject to visual review of real motion footage.

`speech` contains `id`, `speaker`, `provider: "elevenlabs"`, `path`, `start`, `text`, `measured_duration`, optional `gain_db` and `subtitle_position` (`bottom` or `top`). Actual WAV duration is measured, and overlapping dialogue or missing pauses is rejected. `subtitle_segments` are relative `{start,end,text}` entries now derived from the returned ElevenLabs character alignment. With no segments, the entire brief utterance stays visible for its WAV duration. Historical `estimated_duration` is supported solely for explicitly provisional drafts.

The script projects a high-contrast Pillow interface onto measured screen corners. `monitor_aspect_ratio` can specify the actual physical display proportions (2.45 in the reference design); otherwise the average measured quad-edge ratio is used, suitable only for near-frontal shots. The logical raster height and typography adapt to display proportions instead of squashing a tall interface into a wide screen. All five full layouts have been checked at 1.6:1, 2:1, 2.45:1 and 2.5:1. If a wider display cannot fit the copy, the script fails rather than silently clipping; use shorter copy or separate timed pages. Actual readability and projection still require footage review.

Status is fixed at screen top-left, text reveals word by word, completed lines remain visible, and the illustrative footer persists. It never requests readable lettering from a video model. Lower subtitle safe areas are checked against the monitor rectangle; move the subtitle to the top or reframe if they overlap.

```sh
video_demo/.venv/bin/python video_demo/build_film.py --help
video_demo/.venv/bin/python video_demo/build_film.py --monitor-preview video_demo/review/monitor_design_preview.png
video_demo/.venv/bin/python video_demo/build_film.py --write-provisional-srt
video_demo/.venv/bin/python video_demo/build_film.py --write-srt
video_demo/.venv/bin/python video_demo/build_film.py --validate
video_demo/.venv/bin/python video_demo/build_film.py --render
```

Dependencies are Pillow and imageio-ffmpeg (or an ffmpeg executable on PATH); no NumPy is required. A local virtual environment is recommended. `--write-srt` works independently of footage once actual speech is available; use measured/aligned segments for finer phrase boundaries. Rendering strips all generated-source audio, normalizes actual clips to 1080p24, mixes supplied dialogue/ambience, measures and applies two-pass loudness normalization, composites deterministic monitor/title overlays, exports H.264/AAC clean and subtitled MP4s, writes measured `subtitles.srt`, and checks output metadata. No delivery file is written when required footage, speech or review flags are missing.
